01

The story

There is no server.

Two cloud read-later services deleted their users’ libraries within nine months of each other. Laytr’s answer is structural rather than promissory: there is no account to close and no service to shut down, because there is nowhere for a saved item to go except the phone it was saved on.

Everything lives on the device

No account, no sign-in, no Laytr server. Articles are preserved on-device as clean, readable copies that open in Airplane Mode. Screenshots become searchable through on-device Vision text recognition; the images are never sent anywhere for processing.

It comes back when you said

Tonight, this weekend, a date, or Someday. Every choice shows the real time it means before you commit, and the item returns then, staged at the top of the Now tab rather than buried in a list. There are no streaks and no overdue states, and “Not now” is as valid an answer as “Done”.

Built natively, all the way down

SwiftUI throughout, adopting Liquid Glass on iOS 26 through standard components. StoreKit 2 with transaction verification and no third-party SDK anywhere in the purchase path.

02

The two promises

Neither is a policy; both are how the app is built.

These are the two claims worth checking, so here is exactly how far they go.

Lapsing never locks anything

Cancel, or let a plan lapse, and nothing is deleted, hidden or locked. Reading, search, organising, export and item detail take no entitlement at all — there is no path from payment to content to begin with. The only thing that stops is adding new saves, until the archive is back under 30 items or back on a plan.

Export is never paywalled

The whole archive exports as one open ZIP — readable Markdown and HTML for every item, notes, tags, dates and cases, the preserved articles, and original files byte for byte. Free on any plan or none. Restoring a Laytr export is itself exempt from the free limit, because an export you cannot restore is not ownership.

05

Boundaries

What it deliberately does not do.

Most of this is a boundary rather than a gap, and none of it is going to change quietly. The full list, with reasons, is on the support page.

  • No sync. The archive lives on one device, by design.
  • No AI. No summarising, no automatic tagging, no suggestions.
  • No feed, no discovery, no recommendations, no social features.
  • iPhone only, iOS 17 or later, portrait.
  • It never runs JavaScript to save a page, so pages that build themselves in the browser produce no offline copy — and the card says so rather than pretending.
  • It never logs in, never gets past a paywall or a meter, and never downloads or re-hosts video from social platforms.

Brand & contact

Using the artwork.

The wordmark, the compact wordmark, the ✳ reference mark and the app icon are all in the kit, as SVG and PNG. Please use the supplied files rather than recreating them.

  • Clear space on all sides equals one diameter of the ✳ mark at its lockup size.
  • Chartreuse is #C6D92E on light, #D6E84A on dark.
  • Do not recolour the letters, outline, rotate, stretch, add shadows, or place the wordmark over imagery.
  • In running text the name is “Laytr” — capital L, one word. Never “Later”.

Review access

Offer codes for Patron are available to writers and reviewers on request — just say what you are working on. There is nothing to set up: Laytr needs no account, and the free tier is the same app.
